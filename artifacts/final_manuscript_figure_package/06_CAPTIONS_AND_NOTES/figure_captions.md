# Figure Captions

Manuscript-ready captions for all main-text and Supporting Information

## Main text

**Figure 1. Reduced-model (V1) response families across a shared barrier
continuation.** (A) Temperature-dependent monotonic first-passage
continuation from ceramic-like to DBTT-like behavior, plotted as the swept
crack-opening barrier scale (H₀,c) against the shielding coefficient
(χ_shield), colored by regime. (B) The matched cyclic first-passage
continuation over the same parameter range and regime order. (C) S-N-type
initiation response across the matched entropy family, plotted against the
shared entropy-family coordinate Λ_S,ref. (D) Fixed-rate strength-temperature
response generated by the same entropy family, colored by the temperature of
the intermediate-temperature strength maximum where one occurs.
*Evidence class: source-located and structurally matched (topology and
shared parameter grids verified; no manuscript-stated numerical value is
reproduced by this figure).*

**Figure 2. Matched first-passage comparison at the 1x loading rate.** Black
line: V1 analytical prediction. Circles: PF/sharp-front. Squares: FEM/CZM.
Open markers denote incomplete simulation runs for which first passage was
nevertheless recorded. RMS deviations of FEM/CZM from the V1 prediction are
0.23 (ceramic), 0.23 (weakly T-dependent), 1.18 (peak), and 1.33 MPa√m
(DBTT-like). The narrow intermediate-temperature peak in the peak class is
reproduced strongly by the V1 prediction and by PF/sharp-front, but is
attenuated (~31%) in FEM/CZM.
*Evidence class: quantitatively verified.*

**Figure 3. FEM/CZM event-sampled propagation-resistance curves at 500 K.**
Light lines: individual seeds (n=5 per class). Bold line: 5-seed class mean.
Dashed line: saturating fit, K_R(Δa) = K₀ + ΔK_R[1−exp{−(Δa/ℓ_R)^p}]. Inset
values report the mean ± 1 sample standard deviation of K₀, K_ss, and ΔK_R
across the 5 seeds.
*Evidence class: quantitatively verified.*

**Figure 4. Loading-rate dependence of FEM/CZM first passage and the
rate-matched V1 analytical prediction** for nominal rate factors 0.1×, 1×,
10×, and 100× (θ = 45°). The ceramic and weakly T-dependent classes preserve
their broad trends with rate-dependent offsets. The DBTT-like class's
transition temperature shifts to lower values at lower rate and higher
values at higher rate, confirmed under two independent transition-location
definitions (panel E). For the peak class, the analytical prediction's
narrow intermediate-temperature peak shifts to higher temperature as rate
increases; **FEM/CZM shows no resolved local maximum on the available
temperature grid at any rate** — that is, FEM/CZM never reproduces even a
coarse local maximum in this temperature-sampled response.
*Evidence class: quantitatively verified.*

**Figure 5. Fatigue crack growth and crack formation generated by the shared
barrier architecture.** (A) da/dN vs. ΔK for four representative systems
selected from the six-system atlas (smooth ductile, steep cleavage, shifted
ductile, plastic-shielded); the complete six-system atlas is given in SI
Figure 1. Triangles denote upper-bound censored points. (B) Long-growth
K_J(Δa) curves for all six systems: the rank ordering of the weakest- and
strongest-growing systems is stable across all five common-extension
checkpoints examined. Inset: the growth rate of the plastic-shielded system
differs by approximately 17× between two crystal orientations (30° vs. 45°)
at matched nominal driving force; **this orientation-dependent growth-rate
result is quantitatively verified, but the corresponding geometrical crack-
path deflection is not independently verified**, because portable
crack-path coordinates were not recovered for this branch. (C) Censor-aware
connected-crack S-N response for the blunt-notch problem: cycles to root
connection at 700 and 900 MPa, shielded and unshielded, across all available
seeds; right-pointing open markers denote right-censored calculations (no
finite cycles-to-connection was reached), which are distinguished from
completed observations and are not assigned an arbitrary finite or infinite
lifetime. (D) Representative unshielded and shielded spatial fields (matched
seed and stress). The shielded field shows a broader, more diffuse plastic-
strain and dislocation-density distribution than the narrowly localized
unshielded field; **no cross-image magnitude ratio is reported**, because
the two renders do not share a verified common color-scale normalization.
*Evidence class: A, C quantitatively verified; B (growth-rate component) and
D quantitatively/structurally verified as described above.*

**Figure 6. Cross-phenomenon relationships generated by the shared barrier
landscape.** (A) Categorical-association matrix (Cramér's V) among
temperature-dependent strength, monotonic fracture response, rate-defined
fatigue crack-growth-threshold response, and S-N temperature phenotype,
across six fracture contexts. **Cramér's V is reported as its magnitude
only, and is never negative**; where the underlying relationship is
inverse, that direction is stated here rather than shown as a negative
value. The strongest associations (≈0.79-0.81) occur between temperature-
dependent strength and monotonic fracture class; the fracture class and
threshold-temperature phenotype are also strongly associated (≈0.67-0.71) in
most contexts. Associations with the broad S-N temperature phenotype are
markedly weaker (≈0.05-0.28). (B) Matched-temperature relationship between
monotonic first-passage toughness K_c and the rate-defined fatigue
crack-growth threshold ΔK_th, across 1360 matched observations spanning six
fracture contexts. The pooled log-space Pearson correlation is
r ≈ 0.9865; context-specific values range from 0.958 to 0.999 (see SI
Figure 6 for the six individual context fits). (C) Temperature-specific AUC
values (with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals) for the ability of K_c,
ΔK_th, and a strength-anomaly metric to distinguish endurance-like from
continuous S-N response, using the strict S-N definition (knee-like cases
excluded); the dashed line marks chance-level discrimination (AUC = 0.5).
*Evidence class: quantitatively verified.*

**Figure 7. Cohesive-law mapping, peak class, 900 K.** Comparison of the
EXP-floor barrier form's activation enthalpy H(σ) (A) and velocity proxy
v(σ) (B) against bilinear, exponential, and polynomial cohesive-law fits.
The three fitted forms are nearly coincident with the EXP-floor curve; the
velocity proxy spans approximately 6.5 orders of magnitude over the fitted
stress range. This is a deterministic fit of standard cohesive forms to the
existing EXP-floor curve, not a new physical simulation.
*Evidence class: quantitatively verified.*
