finite-operation normalization, local-limit verification, candidate ownership,

## Option A — local incremental front (recommended)

Treat approach and connection as spatially resolved local advance. Each event
uses the same physical activation-area convention as an ordinary front and a
site multiplicity over the eligible local support. Complete-ligament energy is
retained as a diagnostic/proposal bound, while the rate is assembled from
incremental candidates. This gives the cleanest small/distant-cavity and
ordinary-front limit but may require finer topology resolution across a short
ligament.

## Option B — collective finite ligament

Treat the remaining solid ligament as one coherent activated object. Its rate
uses multiplicity one and a separately justified collective activation area;
the barrier is computed from the finite mechanical operation per newly
separated solid area. This permits a nonlocal connection event but introduces a
physical collective support that cannot be inferred from the 2-D numerical
element area.

No existing evidence authorizes selecting the option that merely produces the
larger cavity effect. Until the author chooses A or B and supplies/approves the
corresponding activation-support convention, P7 conditional-interaction cases
remain blocked. Native cavity transport, growth, restart and topology tests are
not blocked.
